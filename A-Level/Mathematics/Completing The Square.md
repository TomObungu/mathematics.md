# Method 1
From A level maths it possible to complete the square when $a\neq 1$
The steps are as follows
- Rewrite $ax^{2}+bx+c$ in the form $a\left( x^{2}+\frac{b}{a}x \right)+c$
- Complete the square normally inside the brackets of $\left( x^{2}+\frac{b}{a}x \right)$ 
	- $\left( \left( x+\frac{b}{2a} \right)^{2}-\frac{b^{2}}{4a} \right)$ 
- Multiply $\left( \left( x+\frac{b}{2a} \right)^{2}-\frac{b^{2}}{4a} \right)$ by $a$ and reduce the sum of coefficients:

## Example 1
Write $3x^{2}-24x+5$ in the form $a(x+p)^{2}+q$:

$$
\begin{gather*}
3(x^{2}-8x)+5 \\ \\
3((x-4)^{2}-16)+5 \\ \\
3(x-4)^{2}-48+5 \\ \\
3(x-4)^{2}-43
\end{gather*}
$$


We can also use this for the cases when $a$ is a negative constant .e.g $a=-1$. Repeat the process but this time factoring out $a$ that is negative:

## Example 2
Write $-x^{2} -14x+16$ in the form $a(x+p)^{2}+{q}$:

$$
\begin{gather*}
-1(x^{2}+14x)+16 \\ \\ 
-1((x+7)^{2}-49)+16 \\ \\
-(x+7)^{2}+49+16 \\  \\
-(x+7)^{2}+55
\end{gather*}
$$


## Example 3
Write $y=-3x^{2}+6x+7$, in the form $y=a(x+p)^{2}+q$ and sole for $y=0$:
$$
\begin{gather*}
y =-3(x^{2}-2x)+7 \\ \\
= -3((x-1)^{2}-1)+7 \\ \\
= -3(x-1)^{2}+3+7 \\ \\
=-3(x-1)^{2}+10 \\ \\
y = 0 \implies -3(x-1)^{2}+10 = 0 \\ \\
(x-1)^{2}= \frac{10}{3}  \\ \\
x-1 = \pm\sqrt{ \frac{10}{3} } \\  \\
\boxed{x =1 \pm \sqrt{ \frac{10}{3} }}
\end{gather*}
$$
