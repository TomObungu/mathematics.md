Consider a box on a horizontal plane. The coefficient of friction is sufficiently high to prevent sliding. 

In this case, the angle $\theta$ is such that the weight provides a clockwise moment about $A$ to return the box to equilibrium. The box does not topple.

![[Pasted image 20260220093908.png]]


Now $\theta$ has increased to the point that the centre of mass has moved to the other side of $A$. The weight now produces an anti-clockwise moment about with bit nothing to balance it. Therefore due to the overwhelming anti-clockwise moment. The box topples

![[Pasted image 20260220094043.png]]


At a certain value of $\theta$, the centre of mass is directly above $A$. The weight produces no moment, but a slight in crease will cause the box to topple. The box is on the point of toppling.

![[Pasted image 20260220094209.png]]

Therefore:

**An object is on the point of toppling when the centre of mass is directly above the pivot. **

Therefore the vertical weight force will act directly below and through the pivot.
