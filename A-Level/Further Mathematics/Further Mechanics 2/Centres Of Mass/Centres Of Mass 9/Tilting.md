Consider a box on a rough horizontal plane, in equilibrium, with no external forces applied.
![[Pasted image 20260220090329.png]]

Previously, we have used the fact that the box is in translational equilibrium, to say that hence, $R=mg$.

Until now, we have not considered rotational equilibrium of the box. 

Consider $A$ as the pivot. $Mg$  produces a clockwise moment about $A$. This is balanced by an anti-clockwise moment from $R$. 

Since $R=mg$, $R$ must be acting at the same perpendicular distance from $A$ as $mg$, in order to produce the same moment.

Now consider a horizontal force $X$ applied at $B$. Assume that that $\mu$ is sufficient enough such that the box does not slide. There is now an additional anti-clockwise moment due to $X$.
