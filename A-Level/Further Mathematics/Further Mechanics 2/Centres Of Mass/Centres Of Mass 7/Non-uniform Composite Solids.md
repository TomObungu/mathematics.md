You may have to find the centre of mass of a solid made from component solids of different densities. We apply the same approach as with laminas - scaling the volumes by their densities. 

# Example 1 
A key is made from a solid plastic hemisphere, radius $\frac{3}{2}a$, density $\rho$, and a metal cylinder, radius $\frac{1}{2}a$, height $3a$, density $3\rho$. 

The hemisphere and cylinder are centred on the same axis. Find the distance of the centre of mass from O.

![[Pasted image 20260220061523.png]]

Set up the table, except this time multiply the 'volume' of each corresponding object by its density:

| Object      | Hemisphere             | Cylinder                               | Total                  |
| ----------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Volume      | $\frac{9}{4}\pi a^{3}$ | $3\left( \frac{3\pi a^{3}}{4} \right)$ | $\frac{9}{2}\pi a^{3}$ |
| Dist From O | $\frac{57}{16}a$       | $\frac{3}{2}a$                         | $\bar{y}$              |

Setting up the formula:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\frac{9}{4}\pi a^{3}\left( \frac{57}{16}a \right) + \frac{9}{4}\pi a^{3}\left( \frac{3}{2}a \right) = \frac{9}{2}\pi a^{3}\bar{y} \\ \\
\frac{729}{64}a = \frac{9}{2}\bar{y} \\ \\
\bar{y} = \frac{81}{32}a
\end{gather*}
$$
