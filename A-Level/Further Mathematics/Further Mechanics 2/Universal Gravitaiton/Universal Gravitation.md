The heliocentric model of the solar system was proposed by Caperenicus in 1543. According to his model :
- Planetary orbits are circular
- The sun is approximately at the centre of the orbit
- The speed of the planet in the main orbit is constant

In 1609 Kepler analysed astronomical observations made by Brahe to formulate his first two laws of planetary motion:
1. The orbit of a planet is an ellipse with the sun at one focus. 
2.  A line segment joining a planet and the sun sweeps out equal areas during equal points of time. 

In 1687, Newton published the Principia, including mathematical analysis that showed that Kepler's laws were a result of his laws of motion and an inverse square law for gravitational attraction.
