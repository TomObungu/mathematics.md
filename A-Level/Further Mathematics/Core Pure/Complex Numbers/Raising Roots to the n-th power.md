Raising the roots of a complex equation to the $m$-th power follows De Moivre's Theorem, which states that for any complex number $z=re^{i\theta}$, raising it to the power $m$ results in $z^{m} = r^{m}e^{mi\theta}$. 

Geometrically, this transforms every point by scaling it by distance $r  ^{m}$ and multiplies the angle by $m$. 

## Geometric result for an $n$ gon
If you start with the regular vertices of a regular n-gon centered around the origin, raising them to the $m$-th power yields a new set of points whose structure depends on the greatest common divisor of $n$ and $m$.

The number of unique points becomes exactly $\frac{n}{gcd(n,m)}$ points.  Thus she shape gets transferred to a regular $k$-gon where $k = \frac{n}{gcd(n,m)}$.

Each of the new points is the image of exactly $gcd(n,m)$ original vertices.
