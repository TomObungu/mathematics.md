Consider the equation 
$$
(\beta(z-\alpha))^{n} = w
$$

This 

To solve this equation you take the same approach as you would solving a regular equation in the form $z^{n}=w$. But instead take $z$ as the whole equation and find the argument modulus and argument of $w$ 
e.g find $|(\beta(z-a))| = |w|$ and $arg((\beta(z-a))) = arg(w)$. Go through the same process of equating coefficients of $z^{n}e^{ni\theta +2\pi} = |w|e^{arg(w) + 2k\pi}$. You end up getting:
$$
\beta(z-\alpha) = (|w|)^{\frac{1}{h}}e^{\frac{arg(w)+2k\pi}{n}}
$$
Therefore:
$$
z_{1} = \frac{\left( (|w|)^{\frac{1}{h}}e^{\frac{arg(w)+2k\pi}{m}}\right)}{\beta} + \alpha
$$
The corresponding roots will the be $z_{1}\omega$, $z_{1}\omega^{2}$, $z_{1}\omega^{3}$, ... $z^{n-1}$
