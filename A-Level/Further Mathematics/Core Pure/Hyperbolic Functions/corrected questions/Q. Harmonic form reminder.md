This question is much like it's A level maths counterpart, however, you make use of the hyperbolic Pythagorean identity:
$$
\cosh ^{2}A - \sinh ^{2}A = 1
$$
Thus in the step of calculating $R$, instead of $R = \sqrt{ A^{2}+B^{2} }$, you do:
$$
R = \sqrt{ A^{2}-B^{2} }
$$
Other than that everything remains the same, just with hyperbolic functions and Osborne's rule considered where needed:
$$
\begin{gather*}
10\cosh x + 6\sinh x \\ \\
R = \sqrt{ 10^{2}-6^{2} } = 8 \\ \\ 
\end{gather*}
$$
As well as that, recall:
$$
\cosh(A+B) = \cosh A\cosh B+\sinh A \sinh B
$$
Thus:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\cosh a = 10 \qquad \sinh a = 6 \\ \\
\therefore \tanh a = \frac{3}{5} \\ \\
a = \frac{1}{2}\ln\left( \frac{1+\frac{3}{5}}{1-\frac{3}{5}} \right)  = \frac{1}{2} \ln\left( \frac{\frac{8}{5}}{\frac{2}{5}} \right) = \frac{1}{2}\ln(4) = 2\ln 2 \\ 
 a = 0.693 (3 d.p)
\end{gather*}
$$
Therefore:
$$
10\cosh A + 6\sinh B = 8\cosh(x + 0.639)
$$
