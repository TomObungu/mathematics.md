Given roots of polynomial, it is possible to find another polynomial whose roots are a linear transformation of the first. 

# Example 1
For a cubic equation $x^{3} - 2x^{2} + 3x - 4 =0$, find the equation with roots, 
$2\alpha + 1$, $2\beta + 1$, $2\gamma+1$, $2\delta  + 1$.

For this problem you can do $w = 2x + 1$, rearrange to get $x = \frac{w-1}{2}$ and substitute back into the equation and expand. 
Doing so you get a polynomial that looks like:
$$
w^{4}-10w^{3} + 24w^{2} + 98w - 97 = 0
$$
