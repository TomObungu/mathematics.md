A quadratic equation of the form $ax^{2} +bx+c=0$, where $a,b,c$  are integers, can have roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$. 

You can determine the relationship between the coefficients of the terms in the quadratic equation and the values of $\alpha$ and $\beta$

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are roots of the equation $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$ then:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} \\ \\ 
\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}
\end{gather*}
$$

# Example 1
The roots of a quadratic equation are $2x^{2}-5x-4=0$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Without solving the equation, find the values of 
$$
\mathbf{a} \ \alpha+\beta \qquad \mathbf{b} \  \alpha\beta \qquad \mathbf{c} \ \frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{\beta} \qquad \mathbf{d} \ \alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}
$$
a
$$
\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{5}{2}
$$
b
$$
\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} = -\frac{4}{2} = -2
$$
c
$$
\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} = \frac{\beta + \alpha}{\alpha\beta} = \frac{\left(-\frac{5}{2}\right)}{-2}= -\frac{5}{4}
$$
d
$$
\begin{gather*}
a^{2} + \beta^{2}  = (\alpha+\beta)^{2} - 2\alpha\beta  \\ \\ 
= \left( \frac{5}{2} \right)^{2} -2(-2) = \frac{41}{4} 
\end{gather*}
$$
# Example 2 
The roots of a quadratic equation $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$ are $\alpha = -\frac{3}{2}$ and $\beta = \frac{5}{4}$. Find integer values for $a$,$b$ and $c$. :
$$
\begin{gather*}
\alpha + \beta = -\frac{1}{4} = -\frac{b}{a} \\ \\
\alpha\beta = -\frac{15}{8} = \frac{c}{a} \\ \\
\end{gather*}
$$
Notice that each term is the coefficients divided by $a$. Substitute them back into the quadratic:
$$
\begin{gather*}
x^{2} + \frac{1}{4}-\frac{15}{8} = 0  \\ \\
8x^{2}+2x-15 = 0 \\ \\
a = 8, b =2, c = -15
\end{gather*}
$$
