For this question you must realise that the question gives the input as $I = \lambda(2i-j)$ as it is parallel to the vector $(2i-j)$ as it acts in that direction. 

For questions like this when given the impulse in terms of a vector and the kinetic energy you must always take the velocity as $v^{2}=i^{2}+j^{2}$:

Firstly we need to write I in terms of $\lambda$ using $I=m(v-u)$when velocity $u = (-i+2j)$ and rearrange for $v$ to later be used in the kinetic energy equation:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\lambda(2i-j) = \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)(v - (-i+2j)) \\ \\
v = 2\lambda(2i-j)+(-i+2j) \\ \\
v = (4\lambda-1)i+(-2\lambda+2)j
\end{gather*}
$$

Forming an equation for KE gives:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\frac{1}{2}m(v^{2}-u^{2}) = 12 \\ \\ 
\frac{1}{2}(0.5)(((4\lambda-1)^{2}+(-2\lambda+2)^{2}) - ((-1)^{2}+(2)^{2})) = 12 \\ \\
\end{gather*}
$$
Solving for $\lambda$ gives:
$$
\lambda = -\frac{6}{5} \quad \lambda = 2
$$
To verify the correct value of $\lambda$ substitute back into the impulse I
$$
\lambda = 0 : \qquad I = \lambda(2i-j) = 0 \\ 
$$
