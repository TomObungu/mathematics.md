# Worked Example 1
A string has a natural length 0.5m and modulus of elasticity $25N$.
A 1kg particle is attached at $B$ and held at a position $0.75m$ below $A$. Calculate it's initial acceleration
![[Pasted image 20251017173635.png]]
This question involves equating the forces in the system using $F=ma$ however you must use the equations for $T$:
$$
\begin{gather*}
T = \frac{25(0.25)}{0.5}=12.5 \\
T - g = a \\ 
12.5 - g = a \\
\therefore a = 2.7ms ^{-1}
\end{gather*}
$$
# Worked Example 2
String AO has length l and modulus $\lambda$. String OB has natural length $2l$ and modulus $2\lambda$. A 2kg particle in equilibirum at O. Find the length of OA

![[Pasted image 20251017192944.png]]
As the system is in equilibrium:
$$
T_{1} = T_{2}
$$
This means we can use the equations for tension to form equations and solve for $x$
$$
\frac{\lambda(x-l)}{l} = \frac{2\lambda(5l-x-2l)}{2l}
$$
This leads to
$$
x-l = 3l - x
$$
and finally $$
x = 2l
$$
![[Pasted image 20251017193243.png]]
The system is now not in equilibrium and instead requires using a new pair of equations for tension and $F=ma$ 
![[Pasted image 20251017193304.png]]
$$
\begin{gather*}
T_{1} = \frac{\lambda\left( \frac{3}{2}l-l \right)}{l} \qquad T_{2}=\frac{2\lambda\left( \frac{7}{2}l-2l \right)}{2l} \\ \\
T_{1} = \frac{\lambda}{2} \quad T_{2}=\frac{3}{2}\lambda
\end{gather*}
$$
Setting up equations for $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\frac{3}{2}\lambda - \frac{\lambda}{2} = 2a \\
\boxed{a = \frac{\lambda}{2}}
\end{gather*}
$$
