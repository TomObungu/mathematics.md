(a) For this question remember that $r=\sqrt{ x+y }$ when given $z=x+iy$ :
$$
r = \sqrt{ 4^{2}+4^{2} } = 4\sqrt{ 2 } 
$$
As well as that $\theta=\arctan\left( \frac{4}{-4})\right)=-\frac{\pi}{4}$. As the range is in $0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi$. $\theta=\frac{3}{4}\pi$
$$
z_{1} = 4\sqrt{ 2 }\left( \cos \frac{3\pi}{4} + i\sin \frac{3\pi}{4} \right)
$$
(b) (i) For this question, if you were to try and convert $3\left( \cos \frac{17\pi}{12} +i\sin \frac{17\pi}{12}\right)$ into Cartesian form you will find that $z=-\frac{3(-\sqrt{ 2 }+\sqrt{ 6 })}{4} + -\frac{3(\sqrt{ 2 }+\sqrt{ 6 })}{4}i$... Now this isn't very easy to work with, especially if you take the root by multiplying the the conjugate when computing $\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}$. So it is better to convert to exponential form instead:
$$
z_{1} = 4\sqrt{ 2 }e^{\frac{3\pi}{4}i} \qquad z_{2} = 3e^{\frac{17\pi}{12}}
$$
Computing $\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}$ gives:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}} = \frac{4\sqrt{ 2 }e^{\frac{3\pi}{4}}}{3e^{\frac{17\pi}{12}}} \\ \\
= \frac{4\sqrt{ 2 }}{3}e^{\frac{3\pi}{4}-\frac{17\pi}{12}} \\ \\
= \frac{4\sqrt{ 2 }}{3}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{3}i} \\ \\
\end{gather*}
$$
From here we can use the calculator to get the Cartesian form:
$$
= -\frac{2\sqrt{ 2 }}{3}-\frac{2\sqrt{ 6 }}{3}i
$$
