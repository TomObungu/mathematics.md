The crucial thing in this question is realise that the tensions below and under the mass are different and the tension below the mass is directed downwards. 
![[Pasted image 20251017150328.png]]

## Why?
Due to the string being extended downwards towards A, past its natural length at B, the tension in the upper segment of the strings naturally points upwards. This causes the particle particle to also pull up at the lower segment of the spring. If the lower segment is being pulled upwards the lower segment of the string exerts a Tension force downwards.

Secondly you must realise that extensions and thereby tensions  in the lower and upper segments of the string are different. As well as that this means the natural length for each segment of the string is "shared" between them and the total length $5a$ is equal to the sum of the extensions plus the natural length of the string:
![[Pasted image 20251017150258.png]]
If the system is in equilibrium, we can therefore form an equation of forces:
$$
\begin{gather*}
T_{1} - mg - T_{2} = 0 \\
\implies T_{1} = mg + T_{2}
\end{gather*}
$$
We can also form an equation for the length of the spring:
$$
\begin{gather*}
x+y+2a = 5a \\ 
x+y=3a \\ 
y = 3a - x
\end{gather*}
$$
Now having an equation of the extension $y$ in terms of $x$, we can now write the equations for $T_{1}$, $T_{2}$ in therms of the equations of tension:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\frac{\lambda(x)}{a} = \frac{\lambda(y)}{a} + mg \\ 
\frac{(2mg)(x)}{a} = \frac{(2mg)(3x-a)}{a} + mg \\
\frac{2(x)}{a} = \frac{2(3x-a)}{a} + 1 \\
2x = 2(3x-a)+a \\ 
2x = 6x -a \\
-4x = -a \\
\therefore x = \frac{1}{4}a
\end{gather*}
$$
Therefore the distance of the particle above the ground is:
$$
\begin{gather*}
y = 3a - \left( \frac{1}{4}a \right) \\
\boxed{y = \frac{11}{4}a}
\end{gather*}
$$
