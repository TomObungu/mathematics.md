In this diagram you need to sketch the vectors and realise a degree rotation of $45^\circ$ will result in the velocity vector being parallel to the $i$ vector or $j$ vector. This is because $4i+4j$ is parallel to the the vector $i+j$ which makes their $i$ and $j$ components the same. This means the deflected vector will be some scalar multiple $a$ of the $i$ or $j$ vector. The different possibilities arise from calculating the pairs of values for $\lambda$ and $\mu$. 
![[Pasted image 20251014095227.png]]
 $$
\begin{gather*}
\text{If v } = ai: \\
 \lambda i + \mu j = 0.5(ai - (4i + 4j)) \\ 
 \lambda i + \mu j = (0.5)(a-4)i - 2j
\end{gather*}
$$
It is possible to then form simultaneous equations using the magnitude of the of I and it's vector.
$$
\begin{gather*}
I = \lambda i + \mu j \\ 
(2.5)^{2} = \lambda^{2} + \mu^{2}
\end{gather*}
$$
For the case of $v=ai$. We can equate coefficients to find out a value of $\mu$. Once this is possible we can substitute the value of $\mu$ back into the equation of magnitude to calculate a value for $\lambda$.

This only works because there is only one variable $a$ in the the equation for impulse. Equating coefficients would not work if both components of the impulse vector contained the $a$ variable:
$$
\begin{gather*}
 \lambda i + \mu j = (0.5)(a-4)i - 2j  \\ 
\implies \mu = 2 \\ \\
 \text{If } \mu = 2: \\
 (2.5)^{2} = \lambda^{2} +2^{2} \implies \lambda = \pm \frac{3}{2}
\end{gather*}
$$
$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{If } v = aj : \\
\lambda i + \mu j = 0.5(aj - (4i+4j)) \\ 
\lambda i + \mu j = 2i +0.5(a-4)j \\ \\
\implies \lambda = 2\\ \\
\text{If } \lambda = 2: \\
(2.5)^{2} = 2^{2} + \mu \implies \mu = \pm \frac{3}{2}
\end{gather*}
$$
This means the possible values of I are:
$$
\begin{gather*}
I = -2i+\frac{3}{2} \\
= -2i -\frac{3}{2} \\
= \frac{3}{2}i -2j \\
= -\frac{3}{2}i -3j
\end{gather*}
$$
Therefore:
$$
\text{There are 4 possibles pairs of } \lambda \text{ and } \mu
$$
