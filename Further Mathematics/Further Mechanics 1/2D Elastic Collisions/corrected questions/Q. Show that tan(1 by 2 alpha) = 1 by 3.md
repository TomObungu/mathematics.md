(a) The trick to this question is to rewrite $\alpha$ in terms of $\frac{1}{2}\alpha$ using the double angle formula and using substitution as $t=\frac{1}{2}\alpha$ to solve for $\alpha$:
![[Pasted image 20251026205406.png]]
$$
\begin{gather*}
v_{1}\cos\beta = v\cos\alpha \\
v_{1}\sin\beta = \frac{2v}{3}\sin\alpha \\  \\
v_{2}\cos \frac{1}{2}\alpha = v_{1}\cos \beta \\ 
v_{2}\sin \frac{1}{2}\alpha = \frac{2v_{1}}{3}\sin\beta \\ \\
\therefore v_{2}\cos \frac{1}{2}~\alpha = v\cos\alpha\\ \\
\therefore v_{2} \sin \frac{1}{2}\alpha = \frac{4}{9}\sin\alpha \\ \\
\therefore \tan \frac{1}{2}\alpha = \frac{4}{9}\tan\alpha 
\end{gather*}
$$
We know that:
$$
\tan\alpha = \frac{2\tan \frac{1}{2}\alpha }{1-\tan ^{2} \frac{1}{2}\alpha}
$$
Therefore:
$$
\tan \frac{1}{2}\alpha = \left( \frac{4}{9} \right)\frac{2\tan \frac{1}{2} \alpha}{1-\tan ^{2} \frac{1}{2}\alpha}
$$
Substituting $t = \frac{1}{2}\alpha$:
$$
\begin{gather*}
t = \left( \frac{4}{9} \right)\frac{2t}{1-t^{2}} \\ \\ 
t(1-t^{2})=\frac{8}{9}t \\ \\
1-t^{2}=\frac{8}{9} \\ \\
t = \frac{1}{3} \\ \\
\end{gather*}
$$
Therefore:
$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}\alpha = \frac{1}{3}}
$$
