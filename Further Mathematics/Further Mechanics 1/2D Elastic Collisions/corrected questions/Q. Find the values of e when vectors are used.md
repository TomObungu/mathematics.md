The question sort of hints at the by stating the mass of the ball.

If the impulse if perpendicular to to AB then you can work out the perpendicular vector for the impulse and that will give you the vector parallel to $AB$.

Finding the impulse:
$$
\begin{gather*}
I = 0.5\left(\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
6
\end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix}
7 \\
2
\end{pmatrix}\right) = \begin{pmatrix}
-3 \\
2
\end{pmatrix}
\end{gather*}
$$
Calculating the perpendicular vector to $AB$:
$$
I_{\perp} = \begin{pmatrix}
-2 \\
-3 
\end{pmatrix} = \vec{AB}
$$
Therefore:
$$
\begin{gather*}
AB = \lambda \begin{pmatrix}
2 \\
3
\end{pmatrix} \qquad \text{Where } \lambda = 1 \\ \\
\therefore \vec{AB} \text{ is  parallel to } (2i+3j) \\ 
QED
\end{gather*} 
$$
For this question the biggest thing you need to remember is the process of finding the unit vectors for the perpendicular component in direction of the wall. Once the perpendicular unit components of $v$ and $v_{1}$ are found, divide  $\frac{v_{1}}{v}$ to get $e$. Remember $e$ has to be positive so you have to take the absolute values of the components
$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{Perpendicular unit vector of wall } \vec{AB} : \\ 
\vec{AB}_{\parallel} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 13 }}\begin{pmatrix}
-3 \\
2
\end{pmatrix} \\  \\
v_{\perp} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 13 }}\begin{pmatrix}
-3 \\
2
\end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
7 \\
2
\end{pmatrix} = -\frac{17\sqrt{ 13 }}{13} \\ \\
v_{1\perp} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 13 }}\begin{pmatrix}
-3 \\
2
\end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
6
\end{pmatrix} = \frac{9\sqrt{ 13 }}{13} \\ \\
e = \frac{\frac{9\sqrt{ 13 }}{13}}{\left|-\frac{17\sqrt{ 13 }}{13}\right|} = \boxed{\frac{9}{17}} 
\end{gather*}
$$
