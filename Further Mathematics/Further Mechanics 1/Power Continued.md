# Worked example 1
It is possible to use WEP with connected particles just like from A level mechanics:
![[Pasted image 20251013082513.png]]
There is a 1000kg car twoing a 200kg trailer up an inclined plan of 20$^\circ$. The car engine has power of 20000. The car is moving at a constant velocity and the car and the trailer experience resistive forces of 400+$4v$ N and 200N respectively. 

For such questions it is possible to form simultaneous equations for both the force in the car and in the trailer

$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{Forces for car : } \\ 
F - 1000g\sin20 - 400 - 6v - T = 0\\
F = 400+4v + T +1000g\sin 20 \\ \\
\text{Forces for trailer } : \\
T - 200g\sin20 - 200 = 0 \\ 
T = 200g\sin20 + 200 \\ \\
 \text{Substitute T for force and use } P=Fv \text{ : } \\
 20000 = (400+6v+200g\sin20 + 200+1000g\sin20)v \\ 
 6v^{2} + (600 + 1200g\sin 20)v - 20000 = 0 \\
 v = 4.30ms ^{-1}
\end{gather*}
$$
# Worked example 2
The towbar now breaks. Using the principle of the conservation of mechanical energy, how much further up the slope does the trailer move before coming to rest? The constant resistive force on the trailer still applies.

For such problems it is important to consider the previous conditions for the problem i.e things like velocity it was, the instant the towbar breaks. Often if they do not explicitly state the velocity immediately after an event occurs, it is safe to use the previous values of velocity. 


![[Pasted image 20251013083703.png]]
$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{Energy at start } = \text{Energy at end} : \\ 
\text{KE} = GPE + W_{F} \\ \\
\frac{1}{2}(200)(4.3)^{2} = 200g(x\sin 20) + 200(x) \\ 
1849 = x(200g\sin20 + 200) \\ 
x = 2.12m
\end{gather*}
$$

# Worked example 3
A roller coaster with mass 500kg is elevated up to 30m and experience a resistive force of 150N. The roller coaster then engages with the ride and goes through a looping section. The start of ride is marked A and the top of the looping section is marked B. The distance AB is 120m. Find the maximum possible height of the looping section at B. 
![[Pasted image 20251013084601.png]]

The maximum possible height is when the velocity at B is 0.

$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{Energy at start } = \text{Energy at end} : \\
GPE = GPE + W_{F} \\ \\
500g(30) = 500g(x) + 120(150) \\
x = 26.3m
\end{gather*}

$$
