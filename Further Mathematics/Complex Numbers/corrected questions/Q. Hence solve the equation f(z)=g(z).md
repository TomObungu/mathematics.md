(a) The possible formations of a cubic function are 3 real roots or 1 complex conjugate pair and 1 real root. This means the straight line can be a single horizontal line on the $\mathrm{Re}$ or a single vertical line in the case when the roots all have the same $\mathrm{Re}$ component. 
(b) In this case, in order for roots to line in a straight line and for their to be complex roots, the $\mathrm{Re}$ part of the roots must all be the same - $\frac{3}{2}$. We know that complex roots come in conjugate pairs thus:
$$
z_{2} = \frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{2}i
$$
and the other root must be real and have the same $\mathrm{Re}$ value as the other complex roots:
$$
z_{3} = \frac{3}{2}
$$
(c) (i) If you were to draw an Argand diagram of the roots of $f(z)$, you will notice that in order for the roots of $g(z)$ to lie on a  **different** straight line and also share a common root is if the line were to intersect horizontally with the vertical line formed by the roots of $f(z)=0$:

Thus in order for horizontal line to intersect on the real axis, there must be a common real solution for $g(z)$. The only real solution of $f(z)$ is $\frac{3}{2}$ thus the common root is:
$$
\frac{3}{2}
$$
(c)(ii) Since we have $z_{1}=-4$ and $z_{2}=\frac{3}{2}$, we can form a quadratic and linear factorisation of the function. The other linear term will be of the form $(z-d)$. From then we can compare coefficients to find the missing root:
$$
\begin{gather*}
(z-(-4))\left( z-\frac{3}{2} \right)(z-d) = z^{3}+Pz^{2}+Qz+12 \\ \\
(z+4)\left( z-\frac{3}{2} \right)(z-d)=z^{3}+{Pz^{2}+Qz} + 12 \\ \\
\left( z^{2}+\frac{5}{2}z -6 \right)(z-d) = z^{3}+Pz^{2}+Qz+12 \\ \\
\end{gather*}
$$
We know that the constant terms in the expansion must multiply to form $12$ thus:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\dots \\ \\
-6(-d) = 12 \\ \\ 
d = 2
\end{gather*}
$$
This gives us the linear factor being $(z-2)$ and thus the other root being:
$$
z_{3} = -2
$$
